## 1. 오프닝 60초 (Andrew가 "tell me about it"으로 시작할 경우)

> "We license FCC — a calcium-carbonate paper filler grown in-situ on a cellulose-nanofibril core, so it bonds to the fiber instead of just sitting in it. That one structural difference removes the trade-off every other filler forces: mills get bulk, strength, stiffness and smoothness together. A top-tier global mill just finished multi-year trials, dropped the incumbent filler tech, and placed a 9,000-ton order — and it's the first filler ever validated in tissue, which opens a segment no incumbent serves. We don't build plants; we license the tech to a filler maker and earn a royalty per ton. Think of it as the CarbonCure model for paper."

→ 30초 안에: 기술 차별점 / 상용 증거 / 비즈니스 모델 / analog. 그다음 Andrew가 파고들 지점을 고르게 둠.

## 2. 예상 질문 12개 + 답변 스크립트

각 항목: **Q** (Andrew가 물을 형태) · *왜 묻는지* · **답변 스크립트(EN)** · 주의.

### Q1. "$1M is small for us — why so little, and is it even enough?" ⭐가장 중요
*Pangaea는 $7M대 Series A를 리드함. 이 질문에 어떻게 답하느냐로 fit이 갈림.*

**A:**
> "It's small on purpose, because this isn't a capital-intensive build. We don't manufacture — the filler maker bears production CAPEX and the mills require zero CAPEX. The $1M does exactly three things to reach royalty inflection: ~$500K to transfer and prosecute the IP into a US holdco, ~$200K to finish tissue validation, ~$300K for a 12-month operating runway. After that, royalties self-fund the business.
>
> That said — we structured a $1M minimum, not a $1M ceiling. Pangaea typically leads larger rounds, and there's a clear use for more: parallel tissue + packaging validation, faster global IP grants, and onboarding multiple mills at once instead of sequentially. If Pangaea wanted to lead a larger round to compress the timeline, we're very open to structuring that together."

주의: 이게 **fit을 살리는 핵심 답변**. "더 큰 라운드면 무엇을 더 살 수 있는가"를 구체적으로 ($2-3M이면 X). $1M 고집 = Pangaea 탈락. 동시에 "아무 금액이나 받겠다"로 들리지 않게 — "minimum, not ceiling" 프레이밍 유지.

### Q2. "Your Year-3 royalty is $22.4M on 2.24M tons. You have 9,000 tons confirmed. Bridge that for me." ⭐가장 중요
*$20M pre의 근거가 Y3 $22.4M이므로, 9,000t → 2.24M t 점프의 신뢰성이 밸류 전체를 떠받침.*

**A:**
> "Fair — let me separate what's contracted from what's modeled, because the model itself is simple. We assume a flat $10 per ton royalty, so revenue is just volume times ten: Year 1 is 290,000 tons → $2.9M, Year 2 is 820,000 tons → $8.2M, Year 3 is 2.24M tons → $22.4M. The only real variable to defend is the volume ramp — the rate isn't aggressive; $10 sits in the middle of the $7.5-$17.5 a ton that 3-5% of a $250-350 price implies.
>
> On the ramp: confirmed today is 9,000 tons with ~10,000 more in motion, so ~19,000 near-term, against a Year-1 model of 290,000. Three layers bridge that:
> 1. The first mill expands FCC across more of its own grades and lines once the trial grade is in production.
> 2. Adoption rides the *licensee's* existing mill customer base — a global filler major already sells to hundreds of mills, so distribution is theirs, not a cold sales build by us.
> 3. Tissue is net-new: first filler ever validated there, ~2M t/yr addressable that no incumbent can serve today.
>
> I'll be direct about the risk: the single biggest assumption is the ramp from ~19,000 contracted tons to that 290,000 Year-1 number. What de-risks it is that the ramp runs through a giant's existing commercial relationships rather than our own sales force."

주의: **모델을 "volume × $10/ton"으로 단순화**해서 제시하는 게 핵심 무기 — Andrew가 검증할 변수를 ramp 하나로 좁혀줌. $10는 one-pager의 "3-5% of $250-350 = $7.5-17.5/ton" 범위의 **중간값**이므로 "공격적 가정 아님"을 먼저 못박을 것. 그래도 Y1 290,000t는 confirmed 9,000t의 ~32배라 Andrew가 가장 세게 칠 지점 — 가능하면 bottom-up(대형 mill 1곳 연간 filler 소비량 × FCC 침투율 = tons)을 한 줄 숫자로 준비. 모르면 "이 ramp가 최대 가정"이라 솔직히 인정하는 게 과장보다 점수 높음.

### Q4. "Omya, Imerys, Specialty Minerals are giants. What stops them from copying or just crushing you?"
*moat / 방어력. advanced-materials VC 필수 질문.*

**A:**
> "Three things. First, IP: the in-situ fiber-mineral bonding is covered by 5 granted patents and 7 pending across Korea, PCT and the US — it's a patented process, not a trade secret they can reverse-engineer freely. Second, their approaches are structurally different and loading-limited — physical mixing for Omya and Imerys, solid particle for Specialty Minerals — so matching bulk-and-strength-together means either infringing or re-architecting. Third, and most important: our go-to-market is to *license to* one of these giants, so the most likely competitor becomes our distribution partner. The first mill already dropped the incumbent filler tech in favor of FCC after multi-year trials — that's the competitive proof."

주의: Andrew는 **FTO(freedom-to-operate)**와 US/EU 특허 강도(아직 pending)를 후속으로 팔 것 → Q11의 patent-transfer 계획으로 연결.

### Q6. "Walk me through the unit economics."
*경제성 우선 thesis. Andrew가 가장 편안해하는 영역.*

**A:**
> "FCC sells at $250-350 a ton. Our royalty is 3-5% of that, so $7.5-$17.5 a ton — and we model it at a flat $10, the midpoint, so the projections aren't leaning on the top of the range. It's near-pure margin, because we carry no production cost. The mill's side: pulp runs $600-800 a ton, FCC replaces a portion at $250-350, so the mill saves on the order of $350 a ton. The filler maker gets a differentiated premium product. Everyone in the chain is better off, which is why we say the substitution is economically forced, not just greener. Contracts run 10-15 years, so it's a long-duration annuity once a mill converts."

주의: **$10/ton flat = 모델 rate** (Q2의 revenue 표와 정확히 정합). "midpoint, not top of range"를 강조해 보수적임을 어필. "economically forced, not just greener" — Andrew의 thesis 문장과 정확히 일치, 반드시 강조.

### Q7. "In a licensing model, what's your actual sales motion? How do you get from one mill to many?"
*licensing 비즈니스의 GTM이 직관에 안 맞아서 자주 묻음.*

In parallel we run direct mill validations like the first one, which create demand pull that the licensee then fulfills. Tissue is the wedge: it's a segment no incumbent serves, so it's the fastest way to expand the licensee relationship into new volume."

### Q8. "Why now? Why hasn't anyone done this before?"
**A:**
> "Two things changed. The enabling tech — growing CaCO3 in-situ on a marine cellulose-nanofibril core — is recent and patented; it's what makes the fiber-mineral bond possible. And tissue validation is genuinely a first; no filler had worked in tissue before. The market signal is that a top-tier mill just dropped incumbent tech for FCC after multi-year trials. On top of that, pulp at $600-800 and the plastic-replacement push make the economics more compelling now than five years ago."
