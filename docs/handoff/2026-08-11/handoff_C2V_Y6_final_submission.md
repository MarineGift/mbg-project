# SECTION 2 — TECHNOLOGY

### Q1. Fundamental operating principles — 150 / 146  ⚠️ **개정**

```
Paper is pulp plus mineral filler. Pulp costs $600 to $800 a tonne against $100 to $250 for filler, but above 25% loading the sheet loses strength: mineral particles block hydrogen bonding between fibres. That ceiling has bound papermaking for decades.

We change the filler's architecture. Cellulose fibrils are dispersed with calcium oxide and ground carbonate in water, then flocculated with ionic polymers. Captured CO2 is injected at 10 to 80 C until the slurry reaches pH 7.0, and calcium carbonate nucleates and grows directly on the fibril surfaces.

Production today uses microfibrillated cellulose supplied by the mill. Our filed route instead generates the fibrils in the same mill that grinds the mineral, the minerals serving as grinding media and alkali source, cutting fibrillation energy sharply.

The product is 80 to 99% mineral by volume, its fibrils integrating into the fibre network rather than interrupting it.
```

### Q2. Tests, demonstrations, pilots, third-party validation — 200 / 195

```
Development ran thirteen years, from a national university laboratory through pilot paper machines to commercial trials.

The decisive validation was multi year testing with a major pulp and paper producer, on that mill's own production machines rather than pilot equipment, concluding 2025. The mill then placed a firm order of 9,000 tonnes annually. That is a purchase decision, not a technical opinion, and the strongest validation available to us.

Commercial production begins September 2026 inside an existing carbonate plant operated by a global top three producer, beside the mill. Bilateral NDAs are in place and the three party confidentiality agreement covering production is in final signature. Neither party installed new reactors.

Performance is published and peer reviewed (ACS Sustainable Chemistry & Engineering 2020; ACS Omega 2022). Against conventional ground calcium carbonate at 25% loading, our filler gives 3.53 km breaking length versus 2.18, bulk of 1.88 versus 1.71 cm3/g, and stiffness of 636 versus 246 Gurley, at 88.8% brightness. At 57.5% ash, far beyond any conventional filler, our sheets still exceed a 33% ash conventional sheet on strength and bulk.

A second global producer, US headquartered, has signed an NDA and is in qualification testing.
```

### Q3. Scale-up roadmap — 200 / 183  ⚠️ **개정**

```
Current operating scale: 9,000 tonnes per year, contracted. Commercial production begins September 2026 inside an existing carbonate plant operated by a global top three producer. We added no equipment.

Three deployment paths, all using plants that already exist.

Path 1 (first order). A carbonate plant beside a mill that operates its own microfibrillated cellulose unit. Both assets already existed.

Path 2 (12 months). Any satellite carbonate plant can make our filler by adding one fibril unit. Quantities are small: about 3 kg of fibril per tonne of product, so 10,000 tonnes needs 30 tonnes of fibril a year. Our two producers operate several dozen plants between them; at 5,000 tonnes each, that is over 300,000 tonnes reachable inside existing infrastructure.

Path 3 (technical objective). Moving to ground carbonate plants, which already own the grinding circuit our process needs. This halves lime use and gives our best carbon performance. We hold laboratory data; plant scale validation is the milestone.

Interim milestones: secure durable point source CO2 supply; validate Path 3 at plant scale; complete US qualification with our second producer; establish first US production.
```

### Q8. Pain point validated through market/customer discovery? — 200 / 171  ⚠️ **개정**

```
The pain point is not one we discovered. It is the industry's defining economic constraint, and mills have tried to solve it for decades.

Pulp costs $600 to $800 per tonne. Conventional filler costs $100 to $250, and ours prices at $250 to $350, still well below pulp. Every mill would rather use more filler. The industry has pursued fibre filler composites, pre flocculation, lumen loading and hybrid carbonates to raise that ceiling, and none has moved it far. That body of work is our validation: willingness to pay has been shown by the money spent trying.

Our own discovery was direct: multi year testing with a mill ended in a 9,000 tonne purchase order at commercial price. A mill will change its furnish for this.

We have also mapped demand: 850 target mills worldwide, each consuming 5,000 to 20,000 tonnes of filler a year.

What we have not validated is the United States. No US mill has run our filler, and closing that gap is a principal reason we apply.
```

### Q9. Unique / differentiating vs competitors and status quo — 200 / 194  ⚠️ **개정**

```
Growing calcium carbonate on cellulose in situ has been attempted repeatedly, and has not reached commercial production. The published routes work in a laboratory and stall on cost, because they require nanocellulose to be prepared first, as a separate upstream operation. That preparation is the expensive part: beating pulp to 100 CSF, then thirty passes through a super mass colloider. Our comparative data put it at 185 kWh per tonne of beating plus 17 MWh per tonne of fibrillation.

We implemented it. We fix the carbonate onto microfibrillated cellulose drawn from a unit the mill already operates, which removes that upstream cost, and we proved both halves of the case at commercial scale: cost falls, because each tonne of our filler displaces a tonne of pulp priced at twice as much, and sheet quality rises rather than degrades, on strength, bulk, stiffness and brightness alike.

Our filed route goes further, merging fibrillation into the milling step that already grinds the mineral, so the fibril becomes a byproduct rather than a purchased input. That halves lime consumption and is our next milestone.

Conventional filler forces a trade between cost and quality. We remove the trade.

# SECTION 3 — CLIMATE IMPACT

### Q1. How does your innovation mitigate climate change? Durability? — 100 / 95

```
Utilization: 431 kg of CO2 is chemically fixed as carbonate per tonne of product. Our feedstock is captured industrial CO2, not merchant gas.

Avoidance: each tonne of our filler displaces roughly a tonne of chemical pulp, whose cradle to gate footprint is 400 to 510 kg CO2. Net avoidance is 220 to 330 kg CO2 per tonne.

Durability: carbonate in paper is stable and does not off gas. Recovered fibre carries it into recycled grades, and landfilled paper retains it indefinitely. Only incineration releases it. We claim durable utilization plus substantial avoidance, not permanent removal.
```

### Q2. Climate impact to date and at scale — methodology — 100 / 96

```
To date: 9,000 tonnes ordered, or 1,980 to 2,970 tCO2 avoided annually. At the 300,000 tonnes reachable within our partners' plants, 66,000 to 99,000 tCO2 annually.

Methodology: mass balance on the carbonation reaction, plus attributional accounting of inputs. Key assumptions: 431 kg CO2 fixed per tonne of product, matched to our filed examples; lime carries roughly 1.0 to 1.2 tCO2 per tonne upstream; fibril input is 3 kg per tonne, so fibre energy is negligible; displaced pulp carries 400 to 510 kg CO2 per tonne from published LCA.

The range reflects pulp grade and calcium allocation.
```

### Q3. Has an LCA been performed? — 100 / 98  ⚠️ **개정**

```
No third-party LCA yet; our figures are internal mass balance calculations against published pulp footprints.

Boundary: cradle to gate, mineral inputs to carbonated product at the plant gate, with displaced pulp as an avoided burden.

Inputs per tonne: roughly 600 kg calcium oxide, 3 kg microfibrillated cellulose, 431 kg CO2, water, milling electricity.

What we learned: energy is not the constraint, since the fibril adds under 10 kWh per tonne. Calcium carries nearly the whole footprint, so sourcing it as a byproduct rather than calcined lime sets our result.

An ISO conformant LCA is a twelve month goal.
```

### Q4. MRV approach — 100 / 95

```
Our product is sold by weight, which makes the primary quantity directly measurable: tonnes shipped under contract, reconciled against invoices.

Carbonate content is verified by TAPPI T211 ash determination, an industry standard method our customers already run on incoming material, so the mineral fraction is confirmed by the buyer rather than asserted by us. Sheet ash is measured the same way at the mill.

CO2 consumed is metered at the reactor. Displaced pulp is calculated from furnish records.

We have not yet engaged a third party verifier. Establishing that protocol is a near term objective.

# SECTION 4 — BUSINESS MODEL AND COMMERCIALIZATION

### Q1. High-level business model — 200 / 196  ⚠️ **개정**

```
We license to filler producers and take a royalty on their sales. We hold no inventory, operate no plant, and carry no working capital.

Our customer is the producer, not the mill, and this is easy to get backwards: we do not replace filler, we replace pulp. A mill adopting our product buys the same tonnage of mineral, or more; it buys less wood. The category grows rather than shifts, so incumbents have no volume to defend and every reason to license.

Our terms are a royalty of 5 to 10% of the producer's sales. The producer in turn signs a 10 to 15 year supply contract with the mill, because the satellite plant beside that mill is their capital at risk. Our royalty inherits that duration.

A single mill consumes 5,000 to 20,000 tonnes of filler a year. Ours prices at $250 to $350 a tonne against $100 to $250 for commodity filler, so the producer earns a premium and our royalty is about $15 a tonne. One mill at 10,000 tonnes is roughly $150,000 a year, at no marginal cost.

Our scaling rate is set by our partners' plant count, not our balance sheet.
```

### Q2. Beachhead market and go-to-market — 200 / 186

```
Beachhead: printing and writing grades, copy paper, wallpaper base stock, label stock, supplied through carbonate producers already operating satellite plants at or near mills. This segment runs the highest filler loadings, so the value of raising the ceiling is largest and most immediately legible to a buyer.

Validated: a major pulp and paper producer completed multi-year testing on its own machines and ordered 9,000 tonnes annually. That mill's neighbouring mill has indicated it will adopt if the material performs. A second global filler producer signed an NDA and is running qualification tests.

Go-to-market is deliberately indirect. We license to producers rather than selling to mills, because adoption propagates through the supply chain: once a mill sees the cost saving, it asks its own filler supplier for the same product, and suppliers competing for that account cannot easily decline. One licence reaches every mill that producer serves.

Our current arrangement demonstrates this directly, the order brought our partner a paper mill it had not previously supplied.

We maintain a database of 850 target mills worldwide to sequence expansion by grade, geography, and proximity to concentrated CO2 sources.
```

### Q3. Markets you wish to validate but have not explored — 200 / 197  ⚠️ **개정**

```
Tissue. Filler has never been usable in tissue, the sheet being too thin to carry it, so a 40 to 45 million tonne market has been closed to mineral. Our structure appears to open it. A third party's Korean patent attempt on tissue carbonate was rejected in April 2026 over our own prior art. Testing moves next to a US university laboratory. We want to learn whether the strength gain holds at tissue basis weights and whether softness is preserved.

The United States, and the network beyond it. A second global producer, US headquartered and among the world's largest makers of precipitated calcium carbonate, has signed an NDA and is running qualification testing. If mutual validation completes, production would run in the United States and supply US mills directly. That producer also operates plants across Asia, so one agreement reaches markets we have never touched. What we cannot yet answer is how US mills evaluate a furnish change, and which markets to sequence first.

Non-paper uses. Our filings cover composites and stone paper at 50 to 99% mineral loading, with roughly half the polymer of existing stone paper. We want to learn whether packaging buyers value that.
```
